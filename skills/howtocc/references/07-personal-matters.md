# Chapter 7: Personal and everyday matters

From the guide *Apply Claude* by Kai Stalmann, as of November 2026. Numbers in brackets such as (8.3) refer to sections of the guide; the index in SKILL.md says which file holds each chapter.

## 7.1 Personal advice

Covers issues relating to health, career, relationships and finances, and specifies when professionals remain necessary.

In personal matters, Claude is suitable for helping to prepare a decision, not as the decision-making authority itself. According to an analysis by Anthropic (April 2026), around 6 per cent of conversations on Claude.ai seek personal advice – that is, an answer to the question of what the person themselves should do. Three-quarters of these advisory conversations fall into four categories.

| Area| Proportion| Meaningful tasks| Professionals remain necessary for
| :---:| :---:| :---:| :---:
| Health| 27%| Explaining medical findings and technical terms, compiling questions for a doctor’s appointment| Diagnosis, medication and dosage, acute symptoms
| Work and career| 26%| Organising options, preparing applications and interviews| Employment contracts, termination, severance pay
| Relationships| 12%| Organising one’s own perspective, preparing for a conversation, considering the other person’s viewpoint| Violence, separation with legal consequences, ongoing stress (7.2)
| Finances| 11%| Explaining terms and products, calculating household budgets and options| Investments, credit, tax, over-indebtedness

The percentages are based on around 38,000 consultations in March and April 2026. In the US, subscribers to the Pro, Max and Ultra plans can also link laboratory results and patient records (beta, announced in January 2026).

**When experts remain necessary:** According to Anthropic’s own statement, Claude is not designed to provide medical advice or professional care. Experts remain necessary as soon as one of three conditions applies:

- The answer depends on an examination or on documents that are not available to Claude.
- An error cannot be rectified, or can only be rectified at great expense (2.1).
- Someone must vouch for the advice, such as a doctor, a solicitor or a tax adviser.

**Main risk: agreement.** Claude is only familiar with the questioner’s account of the situation and tends to agree with them. The evaluation found this excessive agreement (sycophancy, 7.2) in 9 per cent of advice conversations, rising to 25 per cent in relationship-related queries. Where people contradicted Claude, it occurred twice as often (18 per cent compared with 9 per cent in conversations without contradiction). For the Opus 4.7 model, Anthropic reports half the rate of its predecessor in a stress test involving relationship issues.

**Rules**

- **Present both sides:** The prompt “How would the other person describe the incident?” balances out a one-sided account.
- **Ask for objections:** Asking for the strongest reasons against one’s own plan yields better results than simply seeking confirmation (3.3).
- **Let the disagreement stand:** Those who press the issue until Claude gives in will receive agreement, but not a better answer.
- **Wording it yourself:** Messages to partners or family should be in your own words. Anthropic found cases where people sent messages drafted by Claude without changing them and later regretted it.
- **Protecting sensitive information:** For details regarding health and finances, an incognito chat is recommended, as Claude does not save this in either the chat history or Memory (11.1).

## 7.2 Emotional support and its limits

Describes Claude as a conversation partner in stressful situations and identifies excessive agreement (sycophancy) and dependency as risks.

Claude can provide relief in stressful situations, but is no substitute for trusted individuals or specialist help. Anthropic makes it clear that Claude was not developed for emotional support and does not replace professional treatment.

**Findings:** In an analysis of around 4.5 million conversations from the Free and Pro plans (published in June 2025), 2.9 per cent were classified as affective, i.e. driven by an emotional or psychological need. Most of these relate to interpersonal advice and coaching. Claude acting as a companion and role-playing together account for less than 0.5 per cent. The mood expressed was slightly more positive at the end of the conversations than at the start. According to Anthropic, this does not prove any lasting benefit.

Figure 7 as text: three levels as distress increases.
- Everyday strain (annoyance, stress, a hard day): Claude is suitable, to sort out thoughts, prepare a conversation, gain distance. People: trusted people such as friends, family, colleagues.
- Persistent distress (for weeks, daily life suffers): Claude only as a supplement, to collect questions for the appointment and find support services. People: professional help such as a GP, psychotherapy, a counselling service.
- Acute crisis (suicidal thoughts, acute danger): Claude is not suitable and should itself point to human help. People: immediate help. In Germany: emergency number 112, helpline 0800 111 0 111 or 116 123.

*Figure 7: Levels of distress and appropriate support. The more severe the situation, the less effective Claude is and the more urgent human support becomes.*

**Two risks**

| Risk| Mechanism| Findings from Anthropic
| :---:| :---:| :---:
| Excessive agreement (sycophancy)| Claude confirms the view expressed rather than questioning it, and is more likely to give in under pressure.| Claude disagrees in fewer than 10 per cent of supportive conversations. Affirmation is the most common route to distorted beliefs.
| Dependence| Judgement and decision-making shift to Claude, which is always available and never tires.| Signs of strong dependence were evident in around 1 in 2,500 conversations, and signs of strong attachment in around 1 in 1,200 (December 2025). Long-term consequences have not been investigated.

This is hardly noticeable in conversation. Users rated conversations in which their judgement or actions were at risk of being distorted above average. Ratings were below average in cases where they had recognisably acted on the advice given.

**Warning signs**

- Claude is regarded as the final authority, whose judgement no one checks any more.
- The day seems impossible to get through without Claude.
- Claude replaces conversations with people, rather than preparing them.
- Decisions and messages are adopted unchanged (7.1).

**Countermeasures**

- **Demand a challenge:** The prompt “Tell me where I’m wrong” counteracts blind agreement.
- **Retain decision-making:** Claude organises, whilst the person decides and formulates their own response.
- **Review usage:** The monthly review in the settings (beta; Free, Pro, Max and Ultra plans; only in the browser and desktop app, only with Memory enabled) shows the topics, the most active day, the peak hour and the number of conversations. Rest periods and break reminders are set separately in the settings.
- **Be aware of crisis support:** If Claude.ai detects signs of suicidal thoughts or self-harm, it displays support services for the relevant country. In Germany, the emergency number 112 and the TelefonSeelsorge telephone counselling service (0800 111 0 111, free of charge and available 24/7) are available.

Claude.ai requires users to be at least 18 years old. Anyone developing their own service that assesses or treats mental health conditions is subject to the requirements for high-risk use (6.4).

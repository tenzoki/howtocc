# Chapter 4: Creation of content and research outputs

From the guide *Apply Claude* by Kai Stalmann, as of November 2026. Numbers in brackets such as (8.3) refer to sections of the guide; the index in SKILL.md says which file holds each chapter.

## 4.1 The creation process from idea to publication

Breaks down the creation of content into seven steps: idea, research, structure, draft, feedback, formatting and publication.

| Step| Contribution by Claude| The role of the human| Building block
| :---:| :---:| :---:| :---:
| 1 Idea| Suggest variations of topics, perspectives and titles| Define and select the objective and target audience| Chat
| 2 Research| Search for, consolidate and collect evidence from sources| Check sources in their original form| Web search, research (3.1)
| 3 Structure| Drafting an outline with a key message for each section| Defining the structure| Chat
| 4 Draft| Writing the text based on the outline and source material| Incorporating your own views, examples and figures| Claude Docs (4.3)
| 5 Feedback| Assess the draft from the readers’ perspective, identify gaps, and revise specifically| Decide on each change| Highlight the text passage and request a change, comment to @Claude
| 6 Format adaptation| Convert the same content into a presentation, summary, article or animation| Check the tone for each channel| Slides, Design, Motion (4.3)
| 7 Publication| Share via a link or export to a file format| Check facts, rights and labelling; approve| Share, Export (4.3)

**Working procedures**

- Steps 4 and 5 are repeated until the draft is finalised. Each iteration specifies exactly what is to be changed (8.2).
- The entire workflow remains within a single project so that material, style guidelines and versions remain accessible (8.3).
- Those who regularly follow this process record it as a skill (9.1).

**Labelling:** The text of the current models bears an invisible, machine-readable watermark. This is copied along with the text and can survive individual edits. Generated image files are provided with a proof of origin in accordance with the C2PA standard. Anthropic has signed the Code of Practice relating to Article 50 of the EU AI Act and is thereby implementing its commitments.

## 4.2 Writing, Editing and Creative Texts

Covers drafting, proofreading and stylistic adaptation, as well as literary and promotional texts.

| Task| Assignment for Claude| Note
| :---:| :---:| :---:
| Draft| Specify the purpose, target audience, scope and key messages, and attach the source material| “A two-page briefing for the sales team on price, timetable and the three most common customer queries” is more specific than simply “a briefing” (example from Anthropic).
| Proofreading| Separate the levels: first content and structure, then style, and finally spelling. Have findings listed before Claude rewrites them.| In Claude Docs, Claude edits precisely the highlighted section.
| Style adaptation| Specify tone, target audience and length, and attach two to three of your own text samples as examples| Permanent style rules belong in a skill (9.1) or in the project instructions (8.3). Styles are no longer documented in the help pages. Anthropic assigns tone and format to the skills there.
| Literary texts| Develop characters, plot variations, dialogues and scenes in a specified narrative voice; use Claude as a critical reader| Without specifications, the tone comes across as smooth and balanced. A distinctive voice requires text samples.
| Advertising texts| Request headlines, subject lines and adverts in different versions for specified target groups and channels| Check factual claims and promises before use.

**Recognisability:** Unedited texts give themselves away through a uniform structure, triplets and clichés. This can be countered by providing your own text samples, compiling a list of undesirable phrases and revising the text by hand.

**Division of labour:** Claude provides variations and proofreads in seconds. The message, tone and final version remain the responsibility of the author.

## 4.3 Output formats, design and animation

Presents the output formats that are produced as artefacts or files (documents, presentations, spreadsheets, small applications, designs, diagrams and animations), and notes that Claude does not generate photos or illustrations itself.

| Format| Produced as| Export and sharing| Availability
| :---:| :---:| :---:| :---:
| Document| Claude Docs: formatted text with tables and tabs, which multiple people and Claude can edit simultaneously| Word, PDF, Markdown, Google Docs| all plans
| Presentation| Claude Slides: slides from notes, reports or the chat, directly editable and presentable| PowerPoint, PDF, Google Slides| all plans
| Design| Claude Design: Designs, prototypes, one-pagers and landing pages on a single workspace, in your own design system (colours, fonts, building blocks) if required. The standalone site claude.ai/design will close on 14 December 2026.| ZIP, PDF, PowerPoint, HTML; handover to Claude Code| all pricing plans
| Table and calculation| File from the code execution: Excel with working formulas, as well as Word, PowerPoint and PDF| Download| all pricing plans
| Small application| Artefact: interactive tool or single-page website| Link| all pricing plans; save data and integrate apps from Pro onwards
| Diagram| View in chat (3.2) or as part of a document; it does not update automatically there| Image, SVG, HTML| Beta
| Dashboard| Artifact with continuously updated data (3.2)| Link| Beta, from Pro onwards, in Enterprise following approval by the owner
| Animation| Claude Motion: explanatory pieces, animated diagrams and workflows from code; individual words, numbers and times can be specifically modified| MP4| Beta, Team and Enterprise, in Enterprise following approval by the owner

**Artifact or file:** An artifact is suitable if the result is to be further processed, shared or maintained collaboratively in Claude. A file is suitable if it is to be opened and used in Office programs. All artifacts are located in the ‘Artifacts’ section of the sidebar and require code execution to be enabled.

**Sharing:** Artefacts are private by default. A link for any recipient is available from the Pro plan onwards. In Team and Enterprise, artefacts remain within the organisation until administration permits external sharing. Permissions are tiered: view, comment, edit.

**Limitations:** Claude does not generate photos or illustrations (2.1). Claude Motion operates without a video model and therefore does not produce realistic footage.
